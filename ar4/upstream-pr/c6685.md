Fifth box, and it shows the penalty, with two things the thread does not have yet: your reproducer and a full vLLM tensor-parallel serve measured on the **same card, same day**, where the in-serve gap is about twice what the reproducer predicts.

**Environment.** 4x Radeon AI PRO R9700 (gfx1201, 0x7551), single-socket Threadripper PRO, every card on PCIe 5 x16. MES firmware **0x91** (same as yours), amdgpu **DKMS 6.19.14** on host kernel **7.0.0-38**, HIP runtime **7.14.60850** (the Radiance vLLM image, torch 2.11.0+rocm7.14). So this host matches your firmware and driver but sits on a 7.0 kernel like the third and fourth boxes.

**Where it bites in a real workload.** On this box a Radiance/vLLM TP=4 serve of a GDN-hybrid 27B lands at spawn in one of two states (the #6347 pattern). Torch profiler on the same request, FULL graph replay, one rank, medians per decode step:

| | fast spawn | slow spawn |
|---|---:|---:|
| idle gap before / after each small kernel (around the all-reduce kernels) | 3.1 / 3.1 µs | **15.2 / 14.7 µs** |
| GPU idle inside the step | 3.3 ms | 10.4 ms |
| step | 16.8 ms | 26.0 ms |

Kernel durations are identical between the two states; the ~1,000 gaps per step are the whole difference. The in-serve gap (+12 µs) is larger than the reproducer's (+5 µs) on the same card. KFD shows 6 queues per worker process at the default limit in this serve, so there are more candidates for pending waits than the reproducer's five streams; whether the penalty scales with how many queues hold waits is something the reproducer could test with `--max-side` above 4.

With `GPU_MAX_HW_QUEUES=1` in the container, every spawn so far has come up in the fast state (2 of 2 after 6 of 8 slow without it), and the configuration that never split showed no throughput change at concurrency 1, 4 or 8 with the flag, which agrees with the no-cost result in #6347.
